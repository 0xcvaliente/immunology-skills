# Careers in antibody biotech

For job ads, applications and interview prep. The user's own background and where their CV lives are in their memory (`job-search-profile`); read it rather than asking again, and keep every claim in an application traceable to something they actually did.

## Claim what is evidenced

A biotech job ad reads as a wall of unfamiliar vocabulary, and the failure mode is borrowing it. Map each requirement to one of three buckets and say which:

1. **Direct evidence** — they have done this thing. Name the project and the outcome.
2. **Transferable evidence** — they have done the same work in another domain. Say what transfers and what does not: data platform work, compliance regimes, stakeholder coordination, vendor management and systems administration all move across industries intact.
3. **Gap** — no evidence. Leave it out of the CV, and in an interview name it as a gap with a learning route rather than a claim.

Never write wet-lab, GxP-regulated or regulatory-submission experience into a document for someone who has not done it. Biotech hiring verifies, and the vocabulary is exactly where a borrowed claim collapses.

## Role families

What each one is actually judged on:

| Family | Judged on |
|---|---|
| Discovery research | Hands-on platform experience, panel triage judgement, publication record |
| Protein science / developability | Analytical breadth, troubleshooting, instrument fluency |
| CMC and process development | Scale-up record, deviation handling, tech transfer |
| Clinical operations | Trial execution, monitoring, site and vendor management |
| Regulatory affairs | Dossier sections authored, agency interactions, knowledge of the route |
| QA / QC | Audit experience, deviation and CAPA systems, specification setting |
| Bioinformatics / computational | Pipelines, repertoire or structure tooling, reproducibility |
| Data engineering and IT | Validated systems, integrations, data integrity, uptime in a regulated setting |
| Market access and commercial | HTA dossiers, payer engagement, tender work |
| Business development | Deal structures, diligence, partnering |
| Project management | Programme-level planning across functions, governance |

## The IT, data and systems angle

A non-lab technologist enters biotech through regulated systems, and these are the terms that get tested:

- **Lab and clinical systems** — ELN and LIMS (Benchling, Dotmatics, LabWare, STARLIMS), SDMS, instrument integration; on the clinical side CTMS, eTMF, EDC (Veeva, Medidata).
- **Computerised system validation** — IQ/OQ/PQ, GAMP 5 risk-based validation, requirements traceability, change control. The core idea: in a regulated environment a system is not "working" until the evidence that it works is documented.
- **Data integrity** — ALCOA+ (attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original, accurate, plus complete, consistent, enduring, available), audit trails, e-signatures under 21 CFR Part 11 (US) and EU GMP Annex 11.
- **GxP** — GLP, GCP, GMP, GDP; which one applies depends on the activity, and saying the right one in an interview signals real familiarity.
- **Privacy** — clinical and health data under GDPR; in Norway additionally the sector norm for information security and privacy in health and care services ("Normen"), plus Helsedataservice for registry data.

Someone with an information-systems background and consultancy experience is a credible candidate for these, and should say so in these words rather than reaching for science vocabulary.

## Employer research

Company facts are volatile — look them up for the specific application rather than recalling a list:

- The company's own site: pipeline page, then the two most recent annual or quarterly reports, then the last six months of press releases. Note the lead asset's target, format and phase.
- Trial registries for what is actually enrolling, which often contradicts a stale pipeline graphic.
- Cluster and association directories for who exists in a region: Oslo Cancer Cluster, Norway Health Tech, Nordic Life Science. For Norway, Brønnøysundregistrene or proff.no give the legal entity, headcount and accounts.
- Job boards plus LinkedIn for the team's actual composition; a site with no quality or regulatory staff has not reached the clinic.

## Interview preparation

1. Characterise the lead asset with the five questions in the main skill. Being able to state target, format, mechanism, indication and differentiation puts a candidate ahead of most applicants for any function.
2. Locate the role in the value chain: what does this person unblock, and whose work arrives at their desk?
3. Prepare three questions that only someone who read the materials could ask — about a programme's next readout, a platform choice, a regulatory step.
4. Rehearse two or three concrete stories with a result attached, and one honest gap with a plan.
5. Check the vocabulary in the ad against the glossary; mirroring a term correctly is an asset, and mirroring one incorrectly is worse than silence.
