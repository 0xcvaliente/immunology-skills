# Development path

## Target to label

| Stage | Work | Rough duration |
|---|---|---|
| Target selection and validation | Genetics, expression across normal tissue, tractability, competitive check | 6–18 months |
| Discovery campaign | Immunisation or library selection, hit triage, epitope binning | 6–12 months |
| Lead optimisation | Affinity and function tuning, format choice, developability assessment, sequence liability clean-up | 6–18 months |
| Development candidate | One molecule locked, with its sequence, format and intended indication | milestone |
| Cell line and process development | Stable clone, upstream and downstream process, analytics, formulation | 12–18 months |
| IND-enabling studies | GLP toxicology in a cross-reactive species, tissue cross-reactivity, bioanalytical assays, GMP material | 9–18 months |
| Phase 1 | Safety, PK, receptor occupancy, dose escalation to RP2D | 1–2 years |
| Phase 2 | Signal of efficacy, dose and schedule, biomarker-defined population | 1–3 years |
| Phase 3 | Confirmatory, usually randomised against standard of care | 2–4 years |
| Filing and review | BLA or MAA dossier, inspection, review cycles | 1–1.5 years |

Durations are planning ranges, not a programme forecast. Published success-rate analyses (BIO and others) put the probability of reaching approval from phase 1 in the low tens of percent, with biologics above small molecules; cite the current report rather than this file when a number matters.

## Regulatory routes

**United States.** FDA. IND before first dosing; a biologics licence application under section 351(a) of the Public Health Service Act for approval; biosimilars under 351(k). Pre-IND and end-of-phase-2 meetings are where the programme shape is negotiated.

**European Union / EEA.** Clinical trials are authorised per Regulation 536/2014 through CTIS, one application covering the chosen member states. Antibodies take the centralised marketing route: EMA assessment, CHMP opinion, European Commission decision, valid across the EU and extended to the EEA.

**Norway.** Inside the EEA, so an EU centralised authorisation carries across. The national authority is **DMP** (Direktoratet for medisinske produkter / Norwegian Medical Products Agency), which from 1 January 2024 continued and expanded the former Statens legemiddelverk, taking on preparedness, parts of health technology assessment and vaccine procurement; its site is dmp.no. Hospital-funded medicines additionally pass through **Nye metoder**, with tendering by Sykehusinnkjøp — approval and funding are separate hurdles, and a Norwegian market-access question is usually about the second.

**United Kingdom.** MHRA, on its own national route since leaving the EU system.

## Biosimilars

- Approved on a **comparability exercise**: analytical similarity first, then PK and usually one sensitive clinical endpoint — not a repeat of the originator's programme.
- **Totality of evidence** is the governing phrase: no single study decides.
- Indication **extrapolation** can carry approval to indications never studied with the biosimilar, where the mechanism is the same.
- Analytical similarity work is where most of the cost sits, which is why biosimilar developers are judged on CMC capability.

## What kills programmes

- **Efficacy** — the commonest cause. The target was wrong, or engagement did not translate into benefit.
- **Safety** — on-target toxicity in healthy tissue, payload toxicity, cytokine release, immunogenicity.
- **CMC** — low titer, aggregation, viscosity blocking a subcutaneous presentation, impurity profiles.
- **Commercial** — a competitor arrives first, the payer declines, the indication shrinks after a biomarker cut.

Diagnosing which one applies to a given programme means reading the trial record and the company's own disclosures, not inferring from the molecule.
