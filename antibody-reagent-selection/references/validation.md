# Validation

## Reading a datasheet

Work down the page asking what each claim is backed by:

| Field | What to check |
|---|---|
| Clone ID | Present and specific. No clone ID on a monoclonal means the reagent cannot be tracked between vendors or papers. |
| Recombinant or hybridoma | Recombinant means renewable and sequence-defined. |
| Immunogen | A peptide, a domain, a full-length protein, or "proprietary". The immunogen tells you which epitope region to expect, which predicts whether it survives denaturation or fixation and whether it can see the isoform you care about. "Proprietary" is a gap. |
| Reactivity | Which species were **tested** versus **predicted by homology**. Predicted reactivity is a hypothesis. |
| Applications | Each one should have a figure. An application listed without data is a suggestion. |
| Dilution ranges | Wide ranges mean "we did not titrate for your sample"; titration is the user's job regardless. |
| Isotype and host | Needed for secondary antibody choice, isotype controls and multiplexing. |
| Formulation | Carrier protein (BSA blocks in-house conjugation), azide (incompatible with HRP), glycerol, storage temperature. |
| Lot | Record it. For polyclonals, a lot change is a reagent change. |
| Publications | Independent papers using this clone, in your application, are the strongest signal on the page. Check whether the citations actually used this clone, since vendor citation lists are often generated by text matching and are frequently wrong. |

## The five pillars in practice

From Uhlén et al., *Nature Methods* 13:823–827 (2016), proposed by the International Working Group for Antibody Validation and intended to be applied **per application**. The paper's own framing is worth keeping: there are no universally accepted standards, so the pillars are a way to weigh evidence rather than a certificate.

1. **Genetic strategies.** Knockout (CRISPR) or knockdown (siRNA, shRNA) material next to wild type. Signal loss is the strongest specificity evidence available. Caveat: a commercial "knockout" line may retain truncated or residual protein, so verification of the line itself matters, and a partial knockdown giving a partial signal drop is weaker than a clean knockout.
2. **Orthogonal strategies.** Compare the antibody readout against an antibody-free measurement — targeted mass spectrometry, RNA-seq, qPCR, in situ hybridisation. Correlation across a panel of samples with a range of expression is what convinces.
3. **Independent antibodies.** Two or more antibodies to non-overlapping epitopes agreeing on molecular weight, localisation and the pattern across samples. Agreement between two antibodies sharing an epitope region proves little.
4. **Tagged protein expression.** A tagged construct detected in parallel by the target antibody and a tag antibody, at matched positions.
5. **Biological strategies.** Modulate expression by a defined stimulus, differentiation, cell cycle, or inhibitor treatment and show the signal tracks. For phospho-specific antibodies this is mandatory: stimulation and phosphatase treatment, not just a single band.

Phospho-specific and other PTM antibodies carry an extra bar: signal must appear with stimulation, disappear with phosphatase or inhibitor treatment, and be absent in a phospho-site mutant where one is available.

## What a weak validation figure looks like

- A single cropped band with no molecular weight ladder or neighbouring lanes.
- Only an overexpression lysate, with no endogenous sample.
- A knockout lane at a different exposure from the wild-type lane.
- IHC on a tissue with no independent evidence that the target is expressed there.
- Flow histograms with no negative population and no control overlay.
- A claim of species cross-reactivity illustrated in only one species.
- "Representative data" where the representative figure is the only figure.

## RRIDs

Register and cite antibodies by **RRID** from the Antibody Registry (`https://antibodyregistry.org`). An RRID identifies the exact reagent across vendors and resellers, which a catalogue number does not, and most journals now expect it in the methods. When assembling a method section, give target, host, clonality, clone, vendor, catalogue number, RRID, dilution and application for every antibody.

Cell lines carry RRIDs too (`RRID:CVCL_...`), assigned through **Cellosaurus** (`https://www.cellosaurus.org`). Because knockout and antigen-negative controls now carry much of the specificity argument, a method section should identify each control line by name and RRID, and note whether the line is authenticated and what its knockout status is — a misidentified or leaky line silently invalidates the control. The RRID portal (`https://rrid.site`) resolves identifiers across antibodies, cell lines, organisms and software.

## Third-party and community evidence

- Independent characterisation initiatives (for example YCharOS, which tests commercial antibodies against knockout controls and publishes the results openly) are worth a search before buying; where such a report exists for your target, it outranks all vendor material.
- Reviews on vendor sites are weak evidence; reviews on independent reagent databases are better; a paper's methods section stating the clone and showing controls is better still.
- **CiteAb** (`https://www.citeab.com`) ranks reagents and cell lines by how often they are cited, which surfaces clones with real published use rather than vendor-only figures — but confirm the citing paper actually used the clone.
- The Human Protein Atlas publishes its own antibody reliability scores alongside staining images, which is useful for IHC targets.
