The challenge
From prediction to real-world therapeutic development
Running a protein model is easy — AlphaFold and OpenFold are a download away. Turning computational predictions into something a drug discovery team would trust is hard.
Build an AI tool for a real antibody-engineering challenge and see how far you can take it in just one day.
Who it's for
Biologist, coder, entrepreneur, or none of the above — if you want to help shape modern medicine, you're welcome here.

FAQ
Good questions
 Who can participate?
Students, researchers, and professionals interested in biotechnology and entrepreneurship applied to biology. We welcome participants from all backgrounds and disciplines — great ideas happen when different areas of expertise come together.
 Do I need to know programming, bioinformatics, or biology?
No! Preparation materials and mentors will help you understand the challenges and contribute meaningfully. You're welcome to use AI and other resources, and we'll encourage teams to combine expertise such as biology and computer science.
 Do I need a team? What is the maximum team size?
No. Come with a team or come alone — we'll help you find one, aiming for a mix of skills and backgrounds. Maximum team size is 5 people.
 What are the prizes?
Prize money of 3,000 USD for the winning team(s), plus:
Experimental validation: winning sequences expressed and tested in vitro by a partner lab, with a full lab report and characterization data
Scientific publication: the possibility to publish your results
Pitch your project to venture capitalists
Have your project featured in partner media
 How are projects judged?
A judge runs your skill on a case your team hasn't seen. We use two scientific and two product criteria:
Judgment (40%) — clear reasoning behind the molecules you chose.
Execution (20%) — a reproducible pipeline in a GitHub repo.
User fit (20%) — what your design is for, and who needs it.
Path to value (20%) — a credible route to real use, for the molecule or the method.
 What do I need to submit at the end of the hackathon?
A pitch/presentation and a GitHub repository link with the materials created during the hackathon. A working prototype strengthens your submission.
 What are the rules on pre-existing work?
All project outputs and repositories must be created during the hackathon. Prior ideas, research or papers may inspire you, but if your project builds on a pre-existing startup or business idea, you must disclose it to the organizers.
 Who owns the results?
Designs submitted for experimental validation are tested by Adaptyv, and the results are published on Proteinbase under the open ODC-BY license. Published data includes the sequence, predicted structure, design method, measurements, and your name and team name. By submitting, you give Anthropic and Adaptyv permission to use and publish your design, but they don't become its owners.
See the full competition terms for details.
 Will food be provided? What about dietary needs?
Yes — coffee, light breakfast and lunch. Dinner details will follow closer to the event. Tell us about allergies or dietary requirements in the registration form.
 What should I bring?
Your laptop, charger, and personal items for a full day. We provide meals, refreshments, and access to computing resources.
 Is accommodation provided?
Unfortunately not. It's a one-day event, so please arrange your own accommodation if needed. We are aware this may limit participation for people traveling from further away, and we're sorry for that.
