# The pitch

The guide frames the product criteria around a five-minute pitch, and the schedule gives about an hour for all presentations. Confirm your slot length at the opening. The judges are scoring four things; a pitch that makes each visible beats one that tells a story.

## Five minutes, mapped to the criteria

| Time | Slide | Shows | Criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:25 | The user and the problem | One named user, one pain, what it costs them today | User fit |
| 0:25–1:10 | What we built | The pipeline as a funnel with real counts (generated, filtered, selected); that it takes a target and objectives as inputs | Execution |
| 1:10–2:40 | How we chose the designs | Epitopes and why; the controls and which metrics separated them; the selection rule; a table of the picks with each one's reason and named risk | **Judgment** |
| 2:40–3:25 | What we can and cannot claim | Predicted versus measured; objectives with no computational proxy; where predictors disagreed and what we did | Judgment |
| 3:25–3:55 | The unseen case | The same pipeline on a second target, unchanged: what worked, what broke | Execution |
| 3:55–4:50 | Path to value | The next experiment and its cost; who would use the method or the molecule; what is defensible (data, workflow, validation record) | Path to value |
| 4:50–5:00 | Close | The repository link, the one sentence to remember | |

Judgment carries 40%, and the longest stretch of the pitch belongs to it.

## What to prepare in advance

- A funnel figure with the real attrition numbers from the log.
- The selection table: name, route, epitope, key metrics with their source, flags, why it is here, named risk.
- One structure image per top design with the intended epitope highlighted and the CDR contacts shown.
- The calibration chart: which metrics separated the controls (AUC) and which did not.
- An excerpt of the decision log that shows a real disagreement and how it was resolved.
- A "what would we test first in the lab" slide.

## Questions to be ready for

- Why did you trust that metric? (Answer with the controls, not with a paper.)
- What is the false-positive rate of your filters, and how do you know?
- What happens on a target for which you have no controls?
- Which objective has no computational check, and what did you do about it?
- What would the first wet-lab experiment be, and what would a failure teach you?
- Who pays for this, and what do they do today instead?
- What is yours that a competitor cannot run in an afternoon?

## Do not

Quote a market size, an approval count or a hit rate you cannot source; say "AI predicts affinity"; present a score without saying where it came from (the guide asks every group to report where each score came from); or leave the business half of the pitch to the last ten minutes.
