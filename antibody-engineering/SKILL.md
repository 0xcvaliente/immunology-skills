# Antibody engineering

Improve a molecule that exists: raise its affinity, stabilise it, make it more soluble, remove liabilities, humanise it, lower its immunogenicity risk. Every score below ranks variants; none measures affinity, so the output is a small panel for the bench and the reasons for it. For a binder made from scratch use antibody-binder-design.

## Working rules

- **Baseline first.** Characterise the starting molecule before proposing a single mutation.
- **Constraints are inputs.** What must not change (epitope, specificity, cross-reactivity, species, format) is written down before any variant is scored.
- **Screen, not verdict.** Computed ΔΔG is noisy. The participant guide puts classical scoring functions at about Pearson 0.3–0.5 on antibody benchmarks, and the authors of Graphinity conclude that experimental data are currently insufficient to predict antibody–antigen ΔΔG robustly. Expect a ranking, not a measurement.
- **Two families.** Rank with at least two independent families: physics (FoldX, Rosetta Flex ddG), structure-conditioned inverse folding (ProteinMPNN, ESM-IF1), sequence language models (ESM-2, AbLang-2, AntiBERTy, IgLM), learned ΔΔG (Graphinity). Agreement is evidence; disagreement is information.
- **Calibrate on known data.** SKEMPI 2.0, AB-Bind and any mutational data you hold show which family tracks measured change for antibodies like yours. A preprint benchmark (AbBiBench) found structure-conditioned inverse folding models tracked affinity better than the other families it compared; treat that as a reason to include one, not as a ranking of tools.
- **A panel, not a list.** What goes to the bench carries controls and a spread of risk (below).
- **Decision log.** One dated entry per decision, with its reason and its risk, as in antibody-binder-design.
- **Scope.** Therapeutic, diagnostic and research antibodies. A request to engineer an antibody to enhance a pathogen's or toxin's harm, or to evade a deployed countermeasure, is out of scope: say so and stop.

## Steps

Each step ends when its *done* line is true.

0. **State the goal and the constraints.** Which property improves, by how much, measured how (SPR, BLI, display, thermal stability, expression, aggregation), and what must not change. Allowed edits (CDRs only, framework allowed, a cap on mutations), species and format. *Done:* a table of objective, measurable condition and non-negotiables; open questions listed for the human.
1. **Characterise the starting molecule.** Number it (ANARCII or ANARCI), assign germline and find somatic mutations (IgBLAST, IMGT/DomainGapAlign), predict or fetch a structure (an experimental one from SAbDab first; otherwise ImmuneBuilder or IgFold), scan liabilities (`vhh_qc.py` for VH/VHH, `references/liabilities.md` otherwise), score humanness (BioPhi/OASis) and developability (TAP, or TNP for a VHH). *Done:* a baseline card with each result, the structure used and its confidence at CDR3.
2. **Model the complex, or decide you do not need one.** Goals that depend on binding need the interface: an experimental complex, else a co-fold (AlphaFold 3, Boltz, Chai-1, ColabFold) or docking with restraints (HADDOCK; ClusPro's antibody mode). Docking and complex prediction are the weakest link, so treat the pose as a hypothesis and test it against anything known (epitope mapping, alanine scan, published mutants). *Done:* at least one complex model, a statement of what supports it, and `interface_contacts.py` for the contacting positions.
3. **Define the mutation space.** Paratope and near-paratope positions for affinity; surface or liability positions for solubility and stability. Leave out the canonical Cys pair, buried structural residues, and for a VHH the hallmark positions 42 and 52 unless evidence says otherwise (`antibody-sequence-bioinformatics` `references/vhh.md`). Exclude substitutions that create a liability motif. *Done:* a list of positions with the reason for each, the allowed residues, and a cap on variants (single, double, or combinations).
4. **Score with two or more families and calibrate.** Run each on a calibration set with known outcomes, record rank correlation and n, and weight accordingly (`antibody-binder-design` `scripts/rank_designs.py --controls --weights-from-controls` does the aggregation: label variants known to improve as `pos` and known harmful ones as `neg` in the controls file, and pass `--min-distance 0`, because variants of one parent are close by construction and the default diversity filter would discard nearly all of them). *Done:* a table of method, correlation and n on the calibration data; variants ranked with the spread between families shown.
5. **Filter for developability and immunogenicity.** Aggregation and solubility (SAP, Aggrescan3D, CamSol, DeepSP), charge and pI, liability motifs, T-cell epitope prediction (NetMHCIIpan, IEDB MHC-II tools) and humanness. For VH and VHH domains run `vhh_qc.py --annotate` on every variant: a variant may not add a high-severity liability that the parent lacks. A variant that gains affinity and a new epitope or aggregation patch is a trade-off to record, not a win. *Done:* an attrition table and, per kept variant, its flags.
6. **If the goal is humanisation,** the loop differs: pick human germline frameworks nearest the parent, graft the CDRs, and keep the back-mutations that preserve binding; or use BioPhi's humanisation route (it includes the Sapiens language model) and compare the two. BioPhi's OASis score measures humanness. A high humanness score does not guarantee low immunogenicity. For a VHH, positions 42 and 52 are reported to matter for stability and binding, so revert them toward human only with evidence. *Done:* a humanised sequence set with humanness, germline identity and the back-mutations listed with the reason for each.
7. **Choose a panel and plan the readout.** Include the parent and, where they exist, one known improving and one known harmful mutation as controls; mix conservative variants with a few exploratory ones; keep the panel small enough to measure properly. Name the assay, its conditions and what result would change the plan (`antibody-reagent-selection` `references/applications.md` for SPR and BLI). *Done:* an ordered panel with the reason and a named risk per variant, and the readout.
8. **After the bench, recalibrate.** Feed the measurements back: which family tracked them, which filter removed a good variant. *Done:* the calibration table updated and the log closed.
